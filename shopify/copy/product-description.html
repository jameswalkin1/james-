Red light therapy you can wear while you get on with your day. HaloScalp is a soft-shell LED cap that delivers dual-band red and near-infrared light to your scalp, with gentle infrared warmth. You put it on, pick a level, and carry on — making coffee, answering emails, walking around the house.

Why a cap, not a helmet

Most light therapy devices are rigid domes you have to sit still under. Sitting still is the reason most people stop after three weeks. HaloScalp is shaped like an ordinary cap, so the session fits into what you were already doing. The treatment people keep up with is the one that doesn't ask them to rearrange their evening.

What you get

  • 100 LEDs across the crown and hairline, in a duckbill cap shape for even coverage.
  • Dual-band light — red and near-infrared, the two wavelengths used in low-level light therapy for the scalp.
  • Infrared warmth — a gentle heat setting, like a warm compress.
  • 3 intensity levels so you can start low and build up as your scalp gets used to it.
  • Soft, flexible shell that sits like a normal cap rather than a piece of equipment.

How to use it

Put it on, choose your level, and wear it. Consistency matters far more than session length — a few sessions a week you actually stick to will serve you better than an intense first fortnight followed by a drawer. Full timings are in the manual in the box.

Straight talk on results

This is the part most sellers skip, so here it is plainly. Light therapy is gradual, not dramatic. HaloScalp is a supportive addition to a scalp care routine — it is not a medical device, not a guaranteed fix, and not a substitute for medical advice. Results vary considerably, and some people will see little or no change. Anyone promising certain regrowth on a fixed timeline is overselling.

If hair loss is causing you real concern, please talk to a GP or dermatologist. There are clinically established treatments, and a light therapy cap is not one of them.

Safety

Uses low-level red and near-infrared LEDs and is designed for ordinary home use. Do not look directly into the LEDs. Check with a doctor first if you have a scalp condition, are photosensitive, take medication that increases light sensitivity, or are pregnant. Not intended for children.

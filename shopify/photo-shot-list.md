# Photo shot list

You only have supplier images. They're workable but they're the same images every
other store selling this cap is using, which is both a conversion problem and a
credibility problem at €79.99.

When a unit arrives, shoot these on a phone. Daylight near a window, no flash,
plain wall. In rough order of value:

1. **Worn, from the side, in a normal room.** Someone wearing it while doing
   something ordinary — kettle, laptop, hallway. This is the entire proposition
   ("wear it while you get on with your day") and no supplier image shows it.
2. **Worn, from behind.** Answers "will people notice" without a word of copy.
3. **In hand, unpowered.** Establishes scale, material and build quality. Supplier
   renders never do.
4. **Lit, in a dim room.** The red glow is the most striking thing about the
   product and the most screenshot-worthy.
5. **Everything from the box, laid flat.** Cap, cable, manual. Kills the "what do
   I actually get" doubt that supplier listings create.
6. **Close-up of the LED array.** Shows the 100 LEDs are real and evenly spread.
7. **Charging.** Proves the cordless claim, which is now the lead benefit.

Then replace the supplier images with these, keeping one or two supplier shots only
if they show something yours don't.

## Video

If you can manage 15 seconds: put it on, walk out of frame doing something normal,
cut to the glow in a dim room. That's a homepage hero and an ad creative in one,
and it will outperform anything written here.

## What not to shoot

No before/after hair comparisons. Meta rejects that category of creative for
hair-loss advertising, and it contradicts the honest positioning the product page
is built on.

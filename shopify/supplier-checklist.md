# Sourcing checklist — Alibaba

## Message to send the supplier

> Hi, I'm interested in your red light therapy cap. Before I order a sample, could
> you confirm:
>
> 1. Is there a **rechargeable battery** version, or does it only run plugged into
>    the mains? If there is a battery version, what is the capacity and how long
>    does it run per charge?
> 2. Exact **LED count**, and the split between 660nm red and 850nm near-infrared.
> 3. **Power output in watts.**
> 4. Timer and treatment modes — how many, and what durations?
> 5. Do you have **CE certification and a Declaration of Conformity** for the EU?
>    Can you send copies? I also need the RoHS test report.
> 6. Is it a **one-piece cap** or an LED insert plus a separate cap?
> 7. **Sample price** including shipping to Ireland, and lead time.
> 8. Price at **10, 20, 50 and 100 units**, and your real minimum order.
> 9. Can you do **custom packaging** (our logo on the box and the manual)? What is
>    the minimum for that, and what does it add per unit?
> 10. HS code and declared value you use for EU shipments.
> 11. Do you hold **stock in a European warehouse** (Poland, Germany, Netherlands)?
>     If so, can you ship single units from there to EU customers, and what is the
>     delivery time?
> 12. Do you offer **one-piece dropshipping** with blind shipping — no supplier
>     branding, invoices or promotional inserts in the parcel? What is the unit
>     price at quantity 1?
> 13. Are you **IOSS registered** for EU shipments? (If not, my customers get
>     charged VAT plus a handling fee at the door, which I cannot accept.)

Ask all of it in one message. How completely and how quickly they answer tells you
more about whether they are worth dealing with than the price does.

## Before paying

- [ ] **Trade Assurance only.** Pay through Alibaba. Never wire to a personal
      account, never move payment to WhatsApp or direct bank transfer. That is where
      essentially all the fraud happens.
- [ ] Supplier has **3+ years** on the platform and Verified Supplier status.
- [ ] CE certificate and **Declaration of Conformity** received and readable — not
      a photo of a certificate for a different product.
- [ ] RoHS report received.
- [ ] Confirmed whether it is battery or mains. In writing.

## What importing makes you responsible for

Buying from outside the EU and selling under your own name makes you the
**importer**. In practice you carry most of what the manufacturer would:

- **CE marking** must be valid, and you must hold the Declaration of Conformity.
- **EMC and Low Voltage / RoHS** conformity for an electrical product.
- **GPSR (EU 2023/988)**, in force since December 2024: an EU responsible person
  with a name and address on the product or packaging, and traceability
  information. Based in Ireland, you can be your own responsible person.
- Your business name and a contact address on the packaging.

Keep the store's **"not a medical device"** line exactly as written. It is what
keeps this product outside medical device regulation, and every claim on the site
has been built around staying on the right side of that line.

## Order sequence

1. **One sample.** €40–70. Confirms cordless, build quality, real LED count.
2. Keep selling the dropshipped unit meanwhile. Find out if anything moves at all.
3. **Small first batch, 10–20 units.** €300–500 of risk, at real delivery speed and
   real margin. Most Alibaba minimums are soft — ask.
4. Scale only after that batch sells through.

Do not order 50+ units before a single sale has happened. That is buying inventory
for demand that has not been demonstrated.

## What changes if you switch to holding stock

| | Dropship (Zendrop) | Hold stock (Alibaba) |
|---|---|---|
| Unit cost | ~€27 | ~€12–20 at 50–100 units |
| Cash up front | none | ~€1,200–1,500 for 50 |
| Delivery to customer | 11–28 days | 1–3 days |
| Packaging | supplier's generic box | yours |
| Cordless option | not stocked | available from the factory |
| Compliance | murky | squarely yours |
| Risk if it does not sell | none | boxes in the spare room |

Delivery speed is the one that matters most. It has been the biggest single
disadvantage against the competitor since the first review, and stock is the only
thing that fixes it.

## On using Alibaba for dropshipping instead of bulk

Possible, but normally a bad trade:

- **You lose the volume price.** The cheap unit cost is a bulk price. At quantity 1
  expect a quote worse than the dropship platforms already charge.
- **Delivery is no faster.** Same parcel, same route, 10–25 days. Only stock held
  locally fixes delivery speed.
- **No automation.** Every order is a manual message to the supplier with the
  customer's address, and a separate payment.
- **VAT at the door.** A parcel entering the EU from China needs the seller to be
  IOSS registered, or the courier bills the customer the VAT plus €10–15 handling
  before releasing it. Legally disclosed in the shipping policy, but commercially
  it is a one-star review every time.
- **Returns have nowhere to go.**

For automated single-unit fulfilment from China, AliExpress is the tool built for
it — but that is the same business as the current dropship setup, not an
improvement on it.

**The exception is question 11.** A supplier with EU warehouse stock can dropship
single units inside the EU: 2–4 day delivery, no customs, no VAT surprise, no cash
tied up. That combination is worth more than a better unit price, and it costs
nothing to ask.
